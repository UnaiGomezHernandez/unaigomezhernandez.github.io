My research connects EU external action and policymaking with debates on geopolitical change, technology, and political contestation.

## Research interests

- **EU external action and EU–China relations:** European institutions, party politics, and political contestation over the EU’s relationship with China.
- **Geoeconomics and economic security:** the relationship between economic policy, international power, and the EU’s external action.
- **Artificial intelligence and AI governance:** the political and policy dimensions of AI, alongside my current focus as a researcher at the European Commission.
- **Strategic communication and policymaking:** political narratives, polarisation, and how political actors frame policy choices.

My doctoral research, completed in June 2026 through a joint PhD at the University of Edinburgh and KU Leuven, examined the influence of radical right populist parties on EU external action towards China in the European Parliament. My wider interests also include European public policy, illiberalism, and the future of the liberal international order.

## Methods

I combine quantitative and qualitative approaches, drawing on **computational social science**, **text-as-data analysis**, **traditional content analysis**, **elite interviews**, and **comparative research**.

My methodological experience includes computational text analysis in R, topic modelling, word frequency analysis, qualitative comparative analysis (QCA), and participant observation. I have also delivered training on analysing polarisation in political texts using R at the University of Edinburgh’s Centre for Data, Culture & Society.

## Publications and policy engagement

My published work spans academic journals, edited volumes, policy research, and public commentary. It includes work in *European Security* and *Global Policy*, a book review in *Europe-Asia Studies*, and contributions to outlets including FEPS, CEIAS, and AMO.
